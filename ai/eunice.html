01Selling our power

What the miners don't use, we sell through a Power Purchase Agreement (PPA).

A PPA is a contract with an electricity supplier that sets the price for every kWh we export. There are five common ways to set that price. Each card shows how the price would have moved on one real day, Monday 7 April 2025.

Why the System Sell Price (SSP) spill suits us

Our miners let us choose when to export. SSP spill is the only contract that rewards that without tying us down.

We choose when to export

No volumes to promise in advance, so no penalties when the miners or the engine change plan.

Paid the grid's real-time price

SSP jumps when the grid is short of power. A site that can switch quickly is paid for catching those moments.

Low, simple fees

A small fixed fee per kWh (we're targeting about 0.1p) instead of the 25–30% commission an aggregator takes.

One day, two prices

The day-ahead price is set the afternoon before; Eunice plans on it. The System Sell Price is worked out after each half hour by Elexon, from what the National Energy System Operator (NESO) paid to balance the grid. Spill pays the SSP on whatever we export.

02Network time bands

Exporting at the busiest times earns a bonus.

Generation Distribution Use of System (gDUoS) credits are paid by our network operator, National Grid Electricity Distribution (NGED), for power we export when the local network is busiest. These are the rates from 1 April 2027, on top of whatever the PPA pays.

03How the miners flex

Our miners can run harder or gentler. Gentler earns more per kWh.

Each MicroBT M63S++ is rated at 7.2 kW. Eunice can push it up to use all our spare power, or ease it down to 6.2 kW, where it works most efficiently. Switch the miners off and every kWh goes to the grid.

Why not always run at the most efficient setting? When power is cheap, using all of it still earns more in total, even at a slightly lower rate per kWh. Each step down only makes sense once the grid pays more than that slice of power earns in the miners.
05Runs, not flickers

Once the miners start, they run for at least 4 hours.

Every stop and start wears pumps, fans and power supplies. So Eunice only starts the miners if the next 8 half hours pay, not just the cheapest one. It plans on the whole day's prices, published the afternoon before.

How the numbers are worked out. 44 MicroBT M63S++ (hydro) miners, rated 7,208 W and 464 TH/s; hash rate scales with power to the power 0.7 (fitted through MicroBT's Normal and Aggressive modes). Underclock floor 6.2 kW (bottom of MicroBT's Low mode). Cooling and controls: 5 kW, plus a dry cooler of up to 25 kW that scales with miner power. "Earned per kWh" is mining income divided by all the energy used, cooling included.

Assumptions. Hashprice $34 or $40 per petahash per day (switch at the top); £1 = $1.34. Spare power 371 kW (Scrivelsby's average export while the engine ran, Mar 2025 – Mar 2026).
