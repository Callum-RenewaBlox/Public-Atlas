So the people who know the plant can see at once what to trust. The process, its twelve steps and the key colours are the developer’s own, from the project pages; the buildings follow the developer’s published planning drawings, used as a reference for their sizes and layout and not reproduced here; the ground and the town come from government open data. The BLOX compound and its links to the plant are RenewaBlox’s proposal, not part of the consented scheme. Everything else is a modelling choice, and this sheet says which.

From the developer’s published material

The project pages, the planning drawings (2020) and the Heat and Power Plan (January 2021) for the Thainstone Integrated Resource Recovery Facility, read in November 2026

  • The process: the twelve numbered steps, from waste reception to water re-use, in the developer’s words lightly edited for length; the key colours for waste, fuel, flue gas, steam, heat, district heating, electricity, ash and water
  • Scale: up to 200,000 tonnes a year of residual municipal and industrial waste; circa 30 MW of electricity exported; heat for a district heat network
  • Layout and sizes: every building, silo, tank and the stack placed and sized from the planning drawings (site plan, elevations, sections and roof plans), which are the developer’s copyright, used as a reference and not reproduced: one long timber-clad building whose roofline follows Bennachie’s profile, the twin fuel silos at its south end, the 70 m stack and the two heat stores at its north end, the heat station with its coolers, the water treatment and tanks, the office, the gabion wall along the river and the pond
  • Inside: a circulating fluidised bed furnace and boiler; heat pumps in the heat station for the district heating; flue gas condensing in the heat recovery building
  • Finishes: timber in its natural colour, profiled metal in RAL 7012 on the cores and the coolers’ housing, white aluminium on the office, solar panels on the hall roofs, as the drawings’ legends give them
  • Emissions and permitting: continuous monitoring to the plant’s SEPA permit; coolers designed to leave no visible plume; bottom ash and APCR treated into aggregate
  • The heat network: not part of the plant’s consent; the network beyond the site is the Inverurie Community Energy Society’s, still to be consented, so the model draws it dashed, as future and indicative

RenewaBlox’s proposal

The BLOX peaker as modelled for this site, November 2026: not part of the consented scheme

  • 28 MW of flexible demand behind the plant’s meter: sixteen 40 ft Winchain hydro-cooled containers of Whatsminer M63S++ hydro miners, each with dry coolers on its roof, in one fenced compound inside the gabion wall east of the reception hall, on the plot the drawings give to the LNG station (left out of this model) and the yard beside it
  • Dispatch: the plant runs at full load either way; when export prices are high its power would go to the grid, and when they are low the same power could run the BLOX
  • Connection: at 13.5 kV from the plant’s switchroom (item 14 on the drawings), buried along the footway, to eight 13.5/0.4 kV transformers on the compound’s spine, with the 13.5 kV switchgear house and a control container
  • Heat: a heat recovery skid in the compound’s north-east corner and an insulated pipe pair, on a low rack inside the wall and buried where it crosses the service road, to the return at the heat station. It could preheat the water returning from the planned heat network ahead of the developer’s heat pumps, with a bypass so the network never depends on the BLOX; the roof coolers would take the rest. This depends on the plant’s heat balance, which only the developer’s engineers can confirm, and on the miners chosen
  • Layout: the apron at the reception hall’s east lorry door kept clear; the plot the developer reserves for later uses left free; the electrical kit on raised plinths, subject to the plant’s flood design
  • Not claimed: the page gives no heat output, homes, power gain, temperature, heat sale, cost or revenue

Assumed: please check

Each is one setting in the model, so a drawing or a decision replaces it directly

  • Heights and rooflines: from the elevations and sections where they are drawn; where they are not (eaves, roof pitches, the heights of the cores and the bag filter house), estimated
  • The screens: the timber screens over the roofs drawn as solid surfaces, not as the open fins of the design
  • Inside the halls: the reception line (after BMH Technology’s published line, the developer’s preferred partner), the furnace, boiler, turbine, condenser, heat pumps and flue gas plant are illustrative, laid out from the developer’s descriptions and usual practice, not from a design
  • The flows: a diagram of the process drawn over the plant, not its pipework; routes and heights chosen so each flow can be seen
  • Routes off site: the export drawn underground to the fence and dashed beyond it, as the grid connection’s route is still open; the heat network’s route beyond the site indicative
  • The BLOX: the container count and size, the compound’s layout, the transformer kiosks, the 13.5 kV route and the skid and pipe pair’s route are RenewaBlox’s working assumptions
  • Details the drawings don’t show: the transformers’ sizes, the stack’s sampling platform, some doors, the access kiosk, the ash lorry, the pens and fences, the lighting at dusk
  • Finishes: the timber’s tone and weathering, the RAL colours as screen approximations, the office’s panel joints
  • The setting: the ground near the site from LiDAR; beyond it, the town’s buildings as simple forms on Ordnance Survey footprints with assumed heights, roofs and colours; the field pattern invented, as Scotland has no open crop map; the container yard as on the March 2025 imagery
  • The light: one sun for the page and the film, from the south and a little higher than an October noon, so the open halls are lit
  • Branding: the RenewaBlox livery and the BLOX marks on the containers and coolers
  • The cutaway: the stills and the film cut the halls at 8 m and keep some far walls standing; the page makes the halls see-through instead
