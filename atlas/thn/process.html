What comes from the developer, and what is assumed

So the people who know the plant can see at once what to trust. The process, its twelve steps and the key colours are the developer’s own, from the project pages for the Integrated Resource Recovery Facility; the massing of the buildings follows the photograph of the planning model on the same pages; the ground is the former paper mill site between the railway and the River Don. The BLOX peaker is RenewaBlox’s proposal. Everything else is a modelling choice, and this sheet says which.

From the developer’s published material

The project overview and location map for the Thainstone Integrated Resource Recovery Facility, read in November 2026

  • The process: the twelve numbered steps, from waste reception to water re-use, in the developer’s words lightly edited for length; the key colours for waste, fuel, flue gas, steam, heat, district heating, electricity and residues
  • Scale: up to 200,000 tonnes a year of residual municipal and industrial waste; circa 30 MW of electricity exported; heat for a district heat network
  • Site: the former paper mill at Kirkwood Commercial Park, Port Elphinstone, between the Aberdeen–Inverness railway and the River Don, with the A96 beyond the railway
  • Massing: the arrangement in the planning model photograph: a tall boiler house with a slender stack at its north end, a turbine hall, the fuel silos and the covered conveyor from a long, low waste reception and processing hall, coolers by the river, the retained commercial units to the north and the container yard to the south
  • Emissions and permitting: continuous monitoring to the plant’s SEPA permit; coolers designed to leave no visible plume; bottom ash and APCR treated into aggregate

RenewaBlox’s proposal

The BLOX peaker model as modelled for this site, November 2026

  • 28 MW of flexible demand behind the plant’s meter: sixteen hydro-cooled BLOX data-centre containers, each with its roof dry cooler, in a fenced compound on the cleared mill ground west of the turbine hall
  • Dispatch: the plant runs at full load throughout; its output goes to the grid when export prices are high and to the BLOX when they are low, so the generator never turns down and never sells cheap
  • Connection: from the plant’s switchroom to the compound’s switchgear, drawn as one cable route; the compound can be built in stages, four containers at a time
  • Heat: the containers reject their heat to air through the dry coolers; recovery into the district heating return is possible and is not drawn

Assumed: please check

Each is one setting in the model, so a drawing or a decision replaces it directly

  • Dimensions: every building, silo and stack is sized by eye from the model photograph against the site’s width; the stack is drawn at 75 m, the boiler house at about 40 m, the reception hall at 70 by 60 m. None is measured from a drawing
  • Positions: the plant sits on the paper mill’s footprint along the river; the BLOX compound, the transformer compound, the ash and water tanks and the heat station are placed where the ground is clear, not where the design puts them
  • Routes: the grid connection westward under the railway and the district heating main north towards the town are drawn as indicative lines only
  • The setting: the river’s curve, the railway, the A96, the trees and the houses are drawn from map imagery by eye, not surveyed; the ground is flat, where the real site falls gently to the river
  • Finishes: the warm vertical cladding and the colours follow the model photograph; the real materials are the architect’s
