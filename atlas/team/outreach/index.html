The offer

One hook, three limits, and the people it is for

The prospect sends one bill and a letter of authority, and gets back a line-by-line audit with a tem quote beside their current deal. Working name: the Bill X-ray. It is the offer you already gave Boardy, made into a product. Aim it at owner-led and finance-led businesses with a half-hourly meter, in three home patches, at the moment something changes.

Correction first

RED is not your edge. What you wrap around the quote is.

tem says many brokers already sell RED. Any broker on tem’s panel can quote the same price and the same RED Plus line. Four things set the Bill X-ray apart.

01 · Forensics beyond price

VAT and CCL treatment, agreed kVA against real peaks, TNUoS band, reactive power, out-of-contract rates. tem’s own tariff page lists most of these as reducible and leaves the work to the customer. You do the work.

02 · Both sides of the market

You bring generators to tem as well as demand. No ordinary broker can show a business the plant its money reaches.

03 · Commission in plain sight

Commission is a bonus for you, not the model. Set one flat rate in p/kWh and print it on page one of every quote.

04 · Speed

An agent crew can turn a bill into an audit in minutes. A traditional broker takes days and a phone call.

Three limits to design around

The meter, the scheme and the calendar

Half-hourly meters only

Roughly 100,000 kWh a year and up

RED needs a half-hourly meter (tem FAQs). Elexon expects about 80% of all meters on half-hourly settlement by October 2026 and the rest by May 2027 (Elexon). Ask tem whether newly migrated smart meters qualify: if yes, the reachable market widens well beyond today’s half-hourly sites.

P442 is shared and exposed

Sell the all-in price and the audit

P442 went live on 27 February 2025 (Elexon). Elexon counts four active ESNAs, and other suppliers sell exempt supply. OVO has told Parliament the exemption shifts levy costs onto other customers (written evidence). Never build a campaign on the scheme alone.

Most prospects are mid-contract

Quote forward, log every end date

Quote forward start dates, as with the June 2027 church tender. Log every contract end date you learn in Watt’s Next. That list becomes next year’s pipeline: the renewal radar.

Triggers and patches

When a cold prospect becomes a warm one, and where

  • Contract end inside 12 months. The only trigger that matters at quote stage. Everything else is a way of finding it.
  • New finance or operations lead in the last 90 days. New hires review supplier contracts. Sales Navigator filters for this.
  • New premises or a new site. A change of tenancy usually means expensive deemed rates.
  • Out-of-contract rates on the bill. The most urgent case you will meet.
  • Public talk about energy cost, solar, EV chargers or net zero. They are already thinking about it.
  • Budget season and 1 April. TNUoS and CCL rates change each April; CCL rises from 0.801p to 0.827p per kWh in April 2027 (tem tariffs).
  • New treasurer, bursar or trustee. The charity and school version of a new FD.

LinkedIn

People accept people. Run it through the founders.

Run LinkedIn through the three founders’ personal profiles, not the company page. The company page only has to pass the credibility check a prospect makes after your request lands. The crew does the research and the drafting; a founder sends every message by hand.

Part 1 · Foundations

Each founder’s profile, the company page and the trust pack

Each founder’s profile

  • Headline says what the reader gets. For example: “I X-ray business electricity bills. Co-CEO at RenewaBlox, tem partner.” Job titles alone earn no clicks.
  • Location matches the patch. Callum’s profile showed Slough on 2 October. Set it to Swansea: local targeting and “I’m 20 minutes from you” both depend on it.
  • Banner carries the offer in one line. “Send one bill. Get a line-by-line audit and a tem quote. Our commission shown in pence.”
  • About section in three short paragraphs. The problem, what you do, how to start.
  • Featured section holds four things. The Bill X-ray page, a sample audit, your best teardown post, the tem partnership post.
  • Get verified and collect two recommendations. An ID badge and a generator’s or client’s recommendation do more than any claim you write yourself.
  • Turn on Open Profile once you have Premium or Sales Navigator, so anyone can message you free.

Company page

  • Tagline and About repeat the offer, and state the facts a cautious FD looks for: tem partner, Energy Ombudsman ADR member (once confirmed), how you are paid.
  • All three of you list RenewaBlox as current employer, with the same wording.
  • Two posts a week, mostly reshares of the founders’ best posts and case studies.
  • Use the page’s monthly invite-to-follow credits on new connections.

Part 2 · Targeting and list building

Build the list outside LinkedIn, then find the person inside it

That keeps the crew doing the heavy research on open data and keeps every action on LinkedIn human.

Start with the network you already have

  • Export your own connections. LinkedIn’s data download gives each of you a file of your first-degree connections. The crew can segment it in minutes: who owns or runs a site with a half-hourly meter, who is an adviser, who is a generator.
  • Message those people first. A warm note to 50 existing connections will outperform 500 cold requests. Ask each one a single question: “Who do you know with an electricity bill over £30,000 a year?”
  • Mine second-degree paths. For every tier 1 account, check who you both know before you send anything. An introduction beats a request.

Three lists that convert better than any filter

  • People who reacted to or commented on your posts. Message within a day.
  • People who viewed your profile after a request or a post.
  • Attendees of local business events you attend. LinkedIn shows the attendee list for events you have joined.

What the crew adds to each account

A five-line research card, built from public sources and stored in Watt’s Next.

  1. What the site is and what probably drives its load.
  2. Evidence of size: floor area, beds, covers, pupils, or reported kWh where published.
  3. The trigger, if there is one.
  4. The hook: which audit finding is most likely for this kind of site.
  5. A drafted connection note and first message in the founder’s voice.

Part 3 · Content engine

Content does the trust work so that messages can stay short

A prospect who receives your request looks at your last three posts before deciding. Those posts should show a real bill, a real finding and a plain statement of how you are paid.

Formats, in order of return

  1. Document carousels. Six to eight slides: the bill, the finding, the fix, the saving, the offer.
  2. Short video. Sixty to ninety seconds, a founder talking over the bill on screen.
  3. Text with one annotated image. The fastest to produce.
  4. A fortnightly newsletter. Call it The Bill X-ray. LinkedIn notifies subscribers of each issue.
  5. A monthly live bill clinic. Thirty minutes, three anonymised bills audited live, promoted as a LinkedIn Event.

Cadence

  • Each founder: two posts a week, Tuesday to Thursday mornings.
  • Each founder: 15 minutes a day commenting on posts by target accounts and local connectors. Comments are the cheapest reach on the platform.
  • Company page: two reshares a week.
  • All three of you engage with each other’s posts in the first hour.

Production line

  • The crew drafts only from real artefacts: bills, quotes, tem’s partner material, Callum’s blog. No invented numbers.
  • Each founder gets drafts in their own voice, trained on their past writing, and edits for ten minutes before posting.
  • Batch six drafts every Sunday. Cobb reviews; a founder approves.
  • Every post ends with the same line: “Send one bill and I’ll do yours.”
  • Tag tem on partnership posts, and ask your partner manager for a joint post or a joint live session. Their audience is far larger than yours today.
Rules.Savings figures are either tem’s, attributed, or your own, evidenced. Never a blend. No customer or generator is named without written permission. No engagement pods and no bought followers: they distort the only signal you need, which is whether target buyers respond.

Three strategies

Where one relationship brings many meters

Cold LinkedIn is a drip. These three plays each use something RenewaBlox has that an ordinary broker does not: agents that can read thousands of filings, generators of its own, and partners who already hold their clients’ bills.

The memo: one page, addressed to a named person

  • Their number, with the source and page it came from.
  • An estimated annual electricity cost, as a range, with the working shown.
  • What RED and RED Plus change, in tem’s words and attributed to tem. No promised saving.
  • The three checks a Bill X-ray would run on a site like theirs.
  • One action: a QR code to the secure upload, or “reply with one bill”.
  • Who you are, how you are paid, and how to opt out.

Delivery: three touches in one week

  1. Post. A printed letter to the named finance lead at the trading address. A letter with the reader’s own filed number in it gets opened. Postal marketing sits outside the email rules, though data protection law still applies to the named person.
  2. LinkedIn. The founder connects and mentions the letter.
  3. Email from the crew. Limited companies and LLPs only, as the Compliance page explains.

Quality gate

  • Use only an explicit electricity figure. SECR totals often mix fuels and cover a whole group; where that is so, say so on the memo.
  • Cobb checks every extraction against its source. A founder samples one memo in five before anything is posted.
  • Present estimates as estimates. Misleading business-to-business claims are unlawful as well as bad for trust.

Where it will and will not work

The largest reporters already have a consultant and a flexible purchasing contract. Aim at the lower end: organisations using roughly 0.5 to 5 GWh a year, where the FD still signs the energy contract personally.

First 30 days.Pick one vertical in the three home patches. Build 200 records. Send 50 memos. Count replies and bills received per 100 memos, then decide whether to scale.

You sign generators as well as customers, and ordinary brokers do not. Each generator you bring to tem is a local landmark, run by someone who knows every business owner for 20 miles and has a commercial reason to help. Build one campaign around each plant.

Why the generator will help

Strategy C · Borrowed trust

Partners who already hold the bills

Recruit 30 advisers who each have 100 business clients, instead of finding 3,000 businesses yourself. The sharpest version starts with accountants and bookkeepers: they already hold a year of every client’s electricity invoices in their bookkeeping apps. With client consent, the crew can X-ray a whole client book overnight.

X-ray the book, in five steps

  1. The practice signs a short partner agreement covering data sharing and how any fee is disclosed.
  2. It emails clients a one-click consent, from a template you supply (in the Toolkit).
  3. For clients who agree, it exports 12 months of electricity invoices from Dext, Hubdoc or AutoEntry to your secure upload.
  4. The crew audits them overnight and returns a ranked list: who has a half-hourly meter, whose contract ends when, and where VAT, CCL or capacity looks wrong.
  5. The accountant and a founder call the top five clients together.

The accountant gets an advisory win with no work, and a quarterly list of client contracts coming up for renewal. You get warm introductions, with the bills already in hand.

Terms that make partners say yes

  • A pilot with no commitment: five bills, results in a week.
  • A co-branded report they can send under their own name.
  • No exclusivity and no targets.
  • Any referral fee printed on the customer’s quote in p/kWh. Accountants answer to their institute on commissions, and many will prefer a client benefit or a charity donation to a fee.
  • A tracked Bill X-ray link per partner, so Watt’s Next attributes every bill.
The affinity model.A sports club or church gets its own bill checked first. If it saves, it has a story, as Gorseinon RFC does with tem. It then introduces the sponsors and members who own businesses. For each meter that switches, the club receives a fixed donation, shown on the customer’s quote. A sponsor board is a list of local businesses with a reason to say yes.

The renewal radar

Every mid-contract prospect becomes a dated opportunity

  1. Every conversation ends with a contract end date, or a note of why there is none.
  2. The crew sets reminders at 12, 6 and 3 months before that date, and records the notice period.
  3. At six months it requests a live tem price and sends the side-by-side, to prospects who agreed to receive it.
  4. Partners receive a quarterly list of their own clients’ upcoming dates.

After a year of LinkedIn outreach alone, on the planning assumptions, the radar should hold well over 100 dated contracts. That calendar is worth more than the first three months of sales.
