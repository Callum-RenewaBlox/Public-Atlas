So the people who know the factory can see at once what to trust. The process steps are British Sugar’s own, in their words; the buildings follow British Sugar’s planning drawings, used as a reference for their sizes and layout and not reproduced here; the ground and the setting come from government open data. The BLOX Peaker Plant and its feed are RenewaBlox’s proposal, not British Sugar’s plan. Everything else is a modelling choice, and this sheet says which.

From British Sugar

British Sugar’s website (how sugar is made; its factories), its news releases, its planning drawings for the steam drying plant (application 24/01844/F) and the Environment Agency’s permit for the site, read in November 2026

  • The process: steps 1 to 7 are British Sugar’s own seven steps from beet to sugar, in their words and their order, condensed; step 8 is their combined heat and power plant, as they list electricity among the factory’s co-products
  • Layout and sizes: the footprints from British Sugar’s drawings WI 00 000 9500 and 9501 (the location and land ownership plan, both sides of the Wissey) and the heights of the named structures from WI 00 000 9502 (the factory elevations), all submitted with application 24/01844/F. The drawings are British Sugar’s and marked confidential: they were used as a reference for sizes and layout only, and are not reproduced
  • The CHP: a gas turbine and a heat recovery steam generator exhausting through the 60 m stack, with a 30 m bypass stack; its steam heats the evaporators and, from the 2026/27 campaign, the new steam dryers (the permit and British Sugar’s news)
  • The surplus: up to about 18 MW of the CHP’s output is not used by the factory and is exported today. This is British Sugar’s figure, given to RenewaBlox in November 2026; the page gives no other figure for the CHP
  • The journey: the new first-effect and juice-run evaporators (2023), the CO₂ plant restarted with Air Liquide (2024), and the steam dryers for the 2026/27 campaign, which take steam from the CHP and replace the gas-fired pulp dryers; named, without figures
  • The grid: UK Power Networks’ Wissington 33 kV substation stands inside the site, on its west side (UK Power Networks’ 2013 CIRED paper; OpenStreetMap as a cross-check); the export leaves on its 33 kV lines, within an export limit

RenewaBlox’s proposal

The BLOX Peaker Plant as modelled for this site, November 2026: not part of British Sugar’s plans

  • Up to about 18 MW of flexible demand behind the export meter: a data centre that would mine Bitcoin, in ten 40 ft Winchain hydro-cooled containers of about 1.75 MW each (about 17.5 MW of IT), with dry coolers on their roofs
  • Where: on a pad raised to the factory’s yard level, in the field just west of the factory’s west fence, about 115 m from the 33 kV substation and at least 28 m clear of every overhead line. No plot inside the factory’s ring flood bank near the substation fits it. Whether the field is British Sugar’s, and its flood design outside the bank, are questions for British Sugar
  • Connection: from British Sugar’s own HV switchroom and export metering, not from UK Power Networks’ 33 kV, by a buried feed to five 4 MVA transformer kiosks, an HV switchgear house and a control container; the 33 kV lines carry the export only
  • Dispatch: the CHP runs for the factory’s heat as it does today. When export prices are high the surplus is exported as now; when they are low the BLOX takes it instead. It never imports from the grid and adds no generating capacity: the peaker is the export
  • A day at Wissington: the shape of one real campaign weekday’s half-hourly GB market index price (Elexon BMRS, autumn 2026), scaled so that no price can be read; the line is where the data centre would earn about the same as the export, from RenewaBlox’s dispatch model (assumed). It shows how the switch would behave, not a forecast
  • Heat: the containers’ heat goes to the dry coolers on their roofs; whether any of it could be offered on site is a question for British Sugar
  • Not claimed: the page gives no price, revenue, cost or saving; those belong in a later BLOX Dispatch view

Assumed: please check

Each is one setting in the model, so a drawing or a decision replaces it directly

  • Heights and roofs: the named structures from the elevations; every other roof from the Environment Agency’s LiDAR (November 2018), flat with a low parapet unless the LiDAR shows a ridge; no doors, windows or cladding detail beyond ribbed panels
  • Tanks and silos: the thick-juice tanks in their two sizes, silos 1 to 6 with their gallery and head house, and silo 7, to the elevations’ heights; the lime kiln’s head and hoist in an assumed form
  • Built since 2018: the steam drying plant from the plan’s planning hatch and the elevations’ height (the LiDAR predates it)
  • Colours: by eye from imagery and varied per building; not British Sugar’s own colour scheme
  • The beet pad: the clamps, lorries and the field clamp placed as a campaign day might have them
  • The 33 kV lines and substation: the lines on the plan’s routes as wood poles about 11 m high; the substation’s switchroom, its 2013 quadrature booster, the gantry and British Sugar’s switchroom and export metering laid out by assumption
  • The flows: a diagram of the process drawn over the roofs, not its pipework; routes and heights chosen so each flow can be seen; beet reception, washing and the flume placed on the factory’s east side
  • The BLOX: the container count, size and layout, the kiosks, the pad and its height, and the feed’s route are RenewaBlox’s working assumptions, to be confirmed with British Sugar and RenewaBlox’s engineers
  • The setting: the fields coloured by the 2025 crop map as an autumn day might show them; trees from the LiDAR as simple forms; buildings beyond the factory from Ordnance Survey footprints
  • The light: one autumn sun for the page; the dusk lighting is assumed
  • Branding: the RenewaBlox livery on the containers
