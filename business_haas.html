How it works

Power in, compute, heat out.

A Bitcoin miner is a computer that turns almost all the electricity it uses into heat. Put it where you need heat, and the same kilowatt-hour does two jobs.

Two ways to deploy

Sized and laid out for your site.

Which layout fits depends on how your building is heated today, how much space you have, and whether you need the heat all year.

A

Large dry coolerhydro-cooled miners, heat carried by water

How
Miners sit in a compact, hydro-cooled BLOX enclosure. A water loop carries their heat to a large dry cooler — a radiator with fans — that warms your space.
Suits
Larger buildings and bigger heat loads; sites where the miners should sit outside the heated space.
Out of season
When you don’t need the heat, it can be sent outside instead.
B

Mining space heatersair-cooled miners, heat straight into the room

How
Air-cooled mining units are placed in the space itself and heat it directly — like a fan heater that computes while it warms.
Suits
Warehouses, workshops, barns and glasshouses with open floor space.
Out of season
Units can be vented outside, turned down or switched off.
Who it suits

Sites with a steady need for heat.

The more hours you need heat, the more of the miners’ output you use.

Warehouses & workshopsLarge volumes, long working hours
Farms & glasshousesGrowing rooms, livestock sheds, drying
Leisure & sportHalls, changing rooms, clubhouses
Commercial premisesOffices and units with long heating seasons
