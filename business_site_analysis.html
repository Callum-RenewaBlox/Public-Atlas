Capacity

Are you paying for capacity you never use?

Your agreed supply capacity is the amount of power your connection is reserved for, and you pay for it whether you use it or not — £2.39 per kVA a month on the tem quote. Set it too high and you pay for headroom you never touch. Set it too low and excess capacity charges apply on the extra, for the whole month you go over.

Timing

Could more of your power run at night?

On the tem quote, night-time power costs 16.898p a kWh against 20.619p in the day — 3.7p less for every kWh you can move. The cheaper hours are set by your meter — often around midnight to 7am. Not everything can move, but some loads rarely need to run in office hours: water heating, vehicle and battery charging, cold-store pre-cooling, batch processes.

Power factor

Are you paying for power that does no work?

Motors, compressors and some older lighting draw reactive power: it does no useful work, but the network still has to carry it. How efficiently a site uses its supply is its power factor, from 0 to 1. Below about 0.95 — reactive power above a third of the energy used, assessed half-hour by half-hour — network operators charge for the excess. It arrives as a ‘reactive power’ pass-through line on your bill, and power factor correction equipment can reduce or remove it.

04
Levies, taxes & bands

Are you paying levies and taxes you don’t need to?

A business electricity bill carries charges set by government and the networks, not by your supplier. Most are right; some are routinely wrong, or missing a relief the site qualifies for. These are the checks we run against your own bills.

Climate Change Levy

We check

A tax on each kWh of business electricity — 0.801p from April 2026. Some supplies are excluded, and sites with a Climate Change Agreement pay 92% less.

The question: is any of your supply excluded, or eligible for a discount you aren’t getting?

VAT rate

We check

Most businesses pay the standard 20%. Qualifying use — residential, a charity’s non-business use, or very low use — is charged at a lower rate, and mixed-use sites can split the bill.

The question: is VAT charged at the right rate for how your power is actually used?

Transmission (TNUoS) band

We check

A fixed transmission charge — £137.11 a month on the tem quote — set by a band based on your connection and agreed capacity.

The question: is your site in the right band? A wrong band can be disputed with your network operator.

EII exemption or RED Plus

We compare

Energy Intensive Industry exemptions and RED Plus can’t be combined: a business receiving EII exemptions isn’t eligible for P442.

The question: which route leaves your business better off?

Your supply · in every tier

The foundation: RED and RED Plus from tem

Most suppliers buy your power on the wholesale market and pass on its costs and markups. Through RED, tem prices your supply against its own portfolio of UK renewable generators instead, with the unit rate fixed for your contract. RED Plus then links a renewable generator to your usage under P442 (exempt supply), so certain non-commodity costs — government scheme levies — aren’t charged on that share of your usage. The saving is credited monthly, as its own line.
